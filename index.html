About P.A.C.E.

A short team practice for the start of work

P.A.C.E. uses four brief steps: Presence, Attune, Connect, and Energy. Teams choose one activity from each step and run them in order.

Why this order

P  Presence

Start with the immediate environment. Looking outward gives the brain current information about where you are.

A  Attune

Notice capacity without asking people to explain or disclose what is behind it.

C  Connect

Use shared rhythm, timing, attention, or movement without requiring personal content.

E  Energy

End by making pace, workload, stopping points, or limits part of the work.

Research and practice

P.A.C.E. draws from work on stress physiology, orienting, co-regulation, coordinated action, conservation of resources, interpersonal neurobiology, and trauma-informed care. The activities are kept brief and practical on purpose.

The science gives us useful ways to understand what may be happening. It does not mean every activity works the same way for every person or every group.

References and foundations

The P.A.C.E. training materials name the following research and practice foundations.

  • Stephen Porges, Polyvagal Theory and autonomic state.
  • Deb Dana, practice applications of co-regulation and Polyvagal Theory.
  • Giacomo Rizzolatti, action observation and the mirror-neuron system.
  • Stevan Hobfoll, Conservation of Resources theory.
  • Joseph LeDoux, threat processing, fear, and orienting.
  • Daniel Siegel, interpersonal neurobiology.
  • SAMHSA, trauma-informed care principles and guidance.

What P.A.C.E. does not require

  • Personal or emotional disclosure
  • Belief in the process
  • Cameras on for every activity
  • Individual regulation before the group begins

Accessibility

Built for different ways of participating

P.A.C.E. is designed to meet WCAG 2.2 Level AA accessibility standards.

In the app

  • Keyboard access and visible keyboard focus
  • Screen-reader labels, headings, landmarks, and status messages
  • Text and controls that reflow on smaller screens and at larger text sizes
  • Color is never the only way a step, choice, or status is identified
  • High-contrast text and controls
  • Large touch targets for primary controls
  • Reduced-motion support when the device requests it
  • Timers do not automatically move the group to the next activity

In the practice

  • Participation is voluntary
  • No personal disclosure is required
  • Many activities work with cameras off
  • Activities can be skipped without explanation

Accessibility is checked in the final production build as well as during design and development. If something in this app creates a barrier, contact info@traumainformedoregon.org.
